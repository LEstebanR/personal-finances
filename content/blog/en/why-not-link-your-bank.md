Many personal finance apps offer "connect your bank" as the easiest way to get started: you hand over your credentials or authorize access, and the app imports every transaction on its own. It sounds great. The problem is what actually happens, technically, behind that convenience.

## What "connecting" actually means

When an app connects to a bank, it usually isn't talking to the bank directly — it's talking to a third-party aggregator (a company that specializes in reading bank data) that in turn has access to your account. That means your full financial picture — not just transactions, but often balances, personal details, and history — passes through one more intermediary you didn't choose and whose terms you probably never read.

Every extra intermediary is one more place that data can leak, get used for advertising, or end up exposed in a breach that has nothing to do with you or your bank.

## Convenience has a quiet cost

The case for linking your bank is real: it saves the work of logging every transaction by hand. But that saved time gets paid for with something you don't see in the moment — the number of people and systems that end up with visibility into your money.

There's also a less obvious effect of full automation: when everything imports itself, you stop looking closely at each transaction. Logging something by hand, even if it takes ten seconds, forces you to notice it. That small bit of friction is, ironically, part of what makes a personal finance habit actually stick.

## The alternative isn't "keep it all in your head"

Not linking your bank doesn't mean going back to a paper notebook or a spreadsheet that goes stale on its own. It means logging is intentional: every income and expense gets recorded because it happened, at the time it happened or shortly after, without any outside third party ever holding your account credentials.

It's a design decision, not a technical limitation — it keeps control of the data in exactly two places: whoever's using the app, and the private database where it's stored.
